Choosing Your Coffee Grind

Home/Blog/Choosing Your Coffee Grind
In this guideWhole bean or groundChoose your brew methodKeep it freshAsk the team

Start with your brewer, not the bag.

The same coffee can taste very different in an espresso machine and a plunger. Grind size changes how quickly water moves through the coffee and how much flavour it extracts. Matching the grind to your brewer gives you a much better starting point.

If you own a suitable grinder, choose whole beans and grind close to brewing. If not, there is nothing wrong with ordering ground coffee. Just tell us how you make it. A bag labelled simply “ground” leaves too much room for a mismatch.

A practical starting point for each method.

Espresso machine

Espresso generally needs a fine grind. The precise setting depends on the machine, basket, dose and coffee. A grinder you can adjust at home gives you more control as the beans change over time. If we grind for you, tell us the machine you use.

Moka pot

A moka pot usually works with a medium fine grind, typically coarser than espresso. Do not assume an espresso setting will suit it. Follow your brewer’s filling and heating instructions, and avoid tamping the grounds.

Filter coffee

Start around a medium grind for many drip and pour over methods. Paper type, brewer shape and batch size all affect the result. If the brew runs too quickly or tastes weak, you may need to go a little finer; a slow, harsh brew may need a coarser setting.

Plunger / French press

A coarser grind is a useful starting point for a plunger. Give the coffee a consistent steep and press gently. If you find a lot of sediment or the cup is unpleasantly bitter, try a different grind and check your brew time.

AeroPress

AeroPress recipes vary widely. A medium fine grind is common, but your recipe is the better reference. Tell us your usual method if you need ground coffee prepared for it.

Taste is the final guide. Change one variable at a time, grind, dose, water or time, so you know what improved the cup.

Keep the bag working for you.

Store coffee in a sealed container or well closed bag, away from heat, light and moisture. Buy a quantity you can comfortably use. For a slower routine, a smaller bag may be more useful than a larger bag that stays open for weeks.

If you switch between brewers, consider keeping whole beans and using an adjustable grinder, rather than trying to make one pre ground size work everywhere.

Not sure? That is what we are here for.

Our shop asks you to choose a grind before adding coffee to your cart. That is deliberate: we do not silently choose a filter grind and hope it suits you. Choose whole bean, espresso, plunger, filter, moka pot or AeroPress, or ask us for help before you order.
