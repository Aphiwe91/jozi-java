Barista or Bean to Cup?

Home/Blog/Barista or Bean to Cup?
In this guideA staffed coffee barA self service stationThe whole setupYour shortlist

A hotel, office or dealership can run either a barista led coffee bar or a self service coffee station. The venue type alone does not tell you which machine to buy. The useful question is: who will make the coffee, and what experience do you want to offer?

Choose a staffed bar for the human experience.

A commercial espresso machine gives a trained barista control over extraction, milk preparation and the service itself. It can make coffee a visible part of your venue’s identity. It also needs people, training and a repeatable workflow.

When planning a staffed bar, discuss your busiest hour, not only your daily total. A machine that handles an easy morning may become a bottleneck during a concentrated rush. Your grinder, workspace and staffing must keep pace too.

  • Who will be trained to make the coffee?
  • How many drinks are ordered during the busiest period?
  • Will the same station handle takeaway and sit down orders?
  • Who will manage cleaning, dial in and opening checks?

Choose bean to cup for a different kind of convenience.

A fully automatic machine can suit a reception area, office kitchen or waiting area where people make their own drinks. The Jura range in our catalogue is a starting point for that conversation.

“No dedicated barista” does not mean “no work”. Someone still needs to replenish beans, manage milk safely, empty waste, clean the machine and follow the manufacturer’s maintenance routine. Make that responsibility part of the plan.

Ask about the drinks you actually need, cleaning routines, milk storage, daily demand and service support. A long feature list is not a substitute for a suitable workflow.

Budget for the setup, not just the machine.

The machine price is only one line in the project. Water treatment, electrical work, plumbing, drainage, refrigeration, a grinder and installation can affect the final scope. Do not assume every site is ready for a commercial machine.

For a roaster, site planning is even more important: ventilation, fuel, power, fire considerations and production space need a proper assessment. A quoted batch size is not a complete specification or a promise of hourly output.

Ask for a current manufacturer sheet and a written quotation covering exactly what is included. Check warranty conditions, installation responsibilities and lead times before committing.

Bring a useful brief to the conversation.

Tell us where the equipment will go, who will use it, your expected peak demand and a realistic budget. Photos and basic measurements of the proposed site can make the first conversation more productive.

Considering asset finance? Check the published CCS criteria before building a plan around it. Meeting the starting criteria is not approval, and the official provider confirms the final rates and terms.
