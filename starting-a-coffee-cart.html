Planning a Coffee Cart

Home/Blog/Planning a Coffee Cart

A Piaggio coffee conversion can put your coffee business into a compact footprint. The exciting part is the build. The important part is making sure the build serves a real, workable operation.

1. Know where you will trade.

An office park, market and event venue have different expectations. Before specifying a vehicle, ask about site permission, access, trading hours, customer flow, storage and whether you can return to the same space reliably.

Check local trading, food handling, health, insurance and vehicle requirements with the appropriate authorities and advisers. Buying a conversion does not grant permission to trade. A busy looking site is not automatically a viable pitch.

2. Plan power and water before choosing equipment.

Ask what electrical supply is available and whether it is suitable for the proposed machine. If you need independent power, the system must be specified around the equipment’s actual requirements, not a rough guess. A qualified installer should assess the final setup.

Plan fresh water storage or a connection, waste water handling, filtration, refrigeration and cleaning. These are operational needs, not optional extras. They also take space that must be accounted for in the layout.

3. Design around the busiest service period.

Imagine making several drinks in a row. Can you reach the grinder, machine, milk, cups and hand off point without awkward movement? Is there space for stock and cleaning materials? Does the counter work for the people who will actually use it?

Your menu affects the layout. A focused coffee offer may need less space and stock than a broader food and drink menu. Decide what you want to serve before adding equipment that makes the cart more complex.

  • Who will make drinks and take payments?
  • How will milk and perishables be stored safely?
  • Where will stock go during travel and service?
  • What is the opening, cleaning and closing routine?

4. Separate build cost from working capital.

A complete budget should distinguish the vehicle, fabrication, equipment, installation and delivery from ongoing costs such as site fees, staffing, beans, milk, cups, maintenance, insurance and payment processing.

Check which items are included in a quoted vehicle or conversion price. Do not add a base vehicle to a complete pop up build price if the vehicle is already included, and do not assume a “from” price includes the coffee equipment you want.

Forecast with conservative assumptions. We do not promise an income, a payback period or guaranteed trading success. Your own costs, permissions and sales will determine the outcome.

5. Treat finance as a separate decision.

The published CCS criteria are for a registered business trading for at least one year, with annual turnover of R1M+ and VAT registration. A new venture may not meet those conditions. Check eligibility before assuming finance will fund the project.

Our build planner helps prepare a useful brief without inventing an all in conversion total. Bring your location, expected demand, available utilities and budget, and we can start a grounded conversation.
